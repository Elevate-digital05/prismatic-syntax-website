What mobile-first web design means for a service business

By Prismatic Syntax · 5 min read

When someone needs a plumber, a physio or an accountant, they usually look on the phone in their hand, not on a laptop. That is where your site gets judged first, and for most local service businesses it is where it gets judged most often. Mobile-first design is the practice of building for that screen before any other.

What "mobile-first" actually means

It is not a desktop site that rearranges itself on a phone. It is a different order of work: the layout is designed at phone width first, and the larger screens are worked out from there. In practice that means:

  • Smallest screen first: start at about 360px wide and scale up, not down.
  • Big enough to tap: buttons and links at least 44px tall, with room between them for a thumb.
  • Readable as it is: body text around 16px, so nobody pinches to zoom.
  • Nothing hover-only: there is no hover on a touchscreen, so every menu and detail has to work with a tap.
  • Light pages: compressed images and few scripts, because a phone on a train is not on office broadband.
  • One obvious next step: call, book or ask, reachable without scrolling back to the top.

Google reads the phone version

Google now indexes and ranks websites using their mobile version, for searches made on any device. If content is hidden or cut down on your phone layout, Google effectively does not see it, and if the mobile pages are slow, rankings suffer on desktop searches as well. On a small business site, mobile performance and SEO have become the same piece of work.

Google's own research found that 53% of mobile visits were abandoned when a page took longer than three seconds to load. The figure is from 2016; phones and patience have not improved enough since to make it irrelevant.

A checklist for your own site

Open your site on your own phone, ideally on mobile data rather than Wi-Fi, and go down this list:

  • The first screen says what you do and where, without scrolling
  • Text is readable without zooming
  • Buttons are large enough to hit first time
  • Nothing scrolls sideways or spills off the edge
  • Tapping the phone number starts a call
  • The contact form is short and brings up the right keyboard for email and phone
  • Pages appear in a couple of seconds, not after a white screen
  • The menu can be opened and closed with one thumb

If you hit more than two problems, the site is probably costing you enquiries you never hear about, because the people who left did not tell you why.
