How much does a small business website cost in 2026?

By Prismatic Syntax · 5 min read

Ask five people what a website costs and you will get five answers, from "fifteen dollars a month" to "fifteen thousand". Both can be right, because they are describing different things. This is what each price range actually buys a small service business, in US dollars, and what it costs to keep the site running afterwards.

The ranges below are the ones we found when we set our own international prices in 2026: what freelancers, studios and agencies in the UK and the US were quoting for the same scope. They are a guide to the market, not a survey.

What actually moves the price

  • Custom design or template: a template is cheaper to start with. A design made for your business costs more, and is the reason the site does not look like the three competitors who bought the same theme.
  • Number of pages: each page is design, build, writing and testing. A twelve-page site is not four times a three-page site, but it is not far off double.
  • What the site has to do: a shop, a booking calendar or a payment step is a different amount of work from a page that lists your services and a phone number.
  • Who writes the words: copy is the most common reason a website launch stalls. Paying for it costs more up front and usually saves a month of waiting.
  • Search work: basic on-page SEO should come with any decent build. Keyword research, content and monthly reporting are ongoing work, priced separately.

What it costs after launch

A website has running costs whoever builds it. If you look after it yourself, hosting for a small business site is typically $10–$30 a month, plus a domain renewal of roughly $15–$40 a year. If you would rather someone else kept it hosted, backed up, secure and up to date, a care plan covers that: ours run from $300 a month, hosting included.

Why the cheapest quote can cost more

A cheap site is not a bad decision on its own. It becomes one when it is slow on a phone, cannot be found on Google, or cannot be changed without going back to the person who built it. Ask any supplier three questions before you sign: do I own the files and the domain at the end, can another developer work on it, and what does it cost per month once it is live.
