Call, form or chat: getting more enquiries from your website

By Prismatic Syntax · 4 min read

A website that gets visits but no enquiries rarely has a traffic problem. More often the visitor wanted to get in touch and the site made it harder than it needed to be: a phone number in an image, a form with eleven fields, a contact page three taps away. People do not struggle through that. They go back to the search results and try the next name.

Offer more than one way in

Different people want different things at the moment they decide to get in touch, and the right mix depends on your customers more than on any rule:

  • A call: for anything urgent, or anyone who wants to hear a person before committing. Make the number a tap-to-call link on mobile, not text they have to copy.
  • A short form: for people browsing at 11pm, or who want to describe the job properly. Name, email or phone, and a message box. Every extra field loses someone.
  • A chat link: WhatsApp is the default messaging app for a lot of customers in the UK and Europe, and a click-to-chat link opens a conversation in one tap. In North America, a text message link often does the same job.
  • Online booking: if your diary is the business, letting people pick a slot themselves beats any of the above.

Where to put them

  • The first screen: one clear action next to the headline, such as "Request a call" or "Book a visit".
  • Next to prices: the moment someone is weighing options is the moment a question comes up.
  • The end of every page: people who read to the bottom are the most interested, so do not make them scroll back up.
  • In the menu on mobile: a call or chat button that is always one tap away.

Setting up a chat link

A WhatsApp click-to-chat link is a plain web address, with an optional message already filled in:

https://wa.me/[your number]?text=[your message]
The number goes in full international format without the plus sign or a leading zero: a UK mobile 07700 900123 becomes 447700900123.

A pre-filled message such as "Hi, I'd like a quote for…" removes the hardest part, which is knowing what to say first.

The part that matters most: replying

  • Say on the site when you reply, and then do it
  • Set an away message for evenings and weekends, so nobody wonders if it worked
  • Send form enquiries to an inbox someone actually checks
  • Test every button yourself, on your phone, after any change to the site

The quickest way to waste a good website is a form that goes to an address nobody reads.
